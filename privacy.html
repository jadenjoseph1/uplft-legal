UPLFT Privacy Policy

Last updated: 23 September 2026

UPLFT is a strength training app. It builds you a session, watches how the session went, and uses that to build the next one. To do that it has to hold information about your body and your training. This policy explains exactly what it holds, why, who else sees it, and how to get rid of it.

1. Who we are

UPLFT is operated by Jaden Joseph, operating as UPLFT, a sole proprietorship based in Victoria, British Columbia, Canada.

For any question about this policy or your data, email hello@uplftapp.com. We answer within 5 business days, and within 30 days for a formal request to see, correct or delete your data.

(If UPLFT is later incorporated, this name and the one in the Terms of Service are the two places that change.)

2. What this policy covers

This policy covers the UPLFT iOS app and the backend services it talks to. It does not cover any other product or service, and it does not cover what another company does with something you choose to send them from inside the app.

3. You must be 18 or older

UPLFT delivers unsupervised loaded strength training. You must be at least 18 and no older than 80 to create an account. The app enforces both ends of that range at sign-up from your date of birth. It is a hard block, not a warning, and there is no way past it. Above 80 we ask you to train with in-person guidance instead.

We do not knowingly collect information from anyone under 18. If we learn that we have, we delete the account and its data. If you believe a minor has an account, email us at hello@uplftapp.com.

4. What we collect

4.1 What you give us

  • Email address. Used to identify your account and send you a sign-in code. We never store a password. UPLFT signs you in with a one-time code, so there is no password to store, leak, or reset.
  • Name and profile photo, if you choose to add them.
  • Date of birth, which we use to check you are 18 and to scale what the app asks of you.
  • Sex, body weight and height. These drive load estimates and calorie targets.
  • Your training, as you log it. Every exercise, set, rep, weight and difficulty rating, along with the date, how long the session took, and whether you finished it.
  • Readiness check-ins. Energy, soreness, which areas are sore, and whether you are ill. These stay on your phone. They shape the session in front of you and are not uploaded, so they are not restored if you sign in on a new device.
  • Sleep. The hours you slept and how you rate the night. Unlike the rest of the check-in, this is uploaded and restored on a new device, because it is part of your history rather than one morning's answer.
  • Injuries and limitations. Areas you flag as painful or injured, and movements you block.
  • Food and drink you log, including items you type, speak, photograph, or scan by barcode, and the calories and macronutrients attached to them.
  • Weigh-ins.
  • Progress photos, if you take them.
  • Your equipment, schedule and goals.
  • Feedback you send us, including any screenshot you attach.

4.2 What the app works out about you

This is information about you that you did not type in. The app generates it, and we are naming it because you would otherwise have no way to know it exists:

  • A judgement of your competence at each movement pattern, and the level it assigns you.
  • Your best recorded performance on each movement, used to choose your next weight.
  • Estimated one-rep maxes, inferred from sets you rated as hard.
  • Whether you are stalling, whether you have earned a step up, and whether your fitness has decayed after a break.

This is used to pick your next session. It is not used for anything else.

4.3 What is collected automatically

  • Crash and performance data, when the app crashes or runs slowly. This includes your device model, OS version, and a technical trace. It is tagged with your account ID so we can tell whether one person is hitting the same crash repeatedly.

We do not run any product analytics. We do not track which screens you visit or how long you spend on them.

5. Health and fitness information

This is the section that matters most, so it is separate.

5.1 What we hold

Exercises performed, sets, reps, load and difficulty ratings; session dates, duration and completion; body weight, height and weigh-ins; readiness inputs (sleep, energy, soreness, illness); injuries, pain flags and movement limitations; food logged with its calories and macronutrients; progress photos; and the engine-derived conclusions listed in §4.2.

5.2 Why

This data drives the engine. It reads what you lifted, how it went, how long ago you did it, and how you are feeling today, then chooses your movements and your loads from that. Without this data the app cannot build you a programme. That is the entire product.

5.3 What we will never do with it

  • We do not sell your health or training data. Not to anyone, ever.
  • We do not share it with advertisers, data brokers, or marketing platforms.
  • We do not use it to target advertising to you, inside this app or anywhere else.
  • We do not share it with insurers or employers.

UPLFT does not read from or write to Apple Health. It holds no HealthKit data.

7. Your consent, and the law we work under

In Canada, PIPEDA requires your meaningful, informed consent. Because UPLFT collects health information, that consent has to be express: asked for specifically, not inferred from your carrying on using the app. So before UPLFT asks you a single question about your body, before the account exists and before anything is collected, it shows you a consent step that names in plain words what health information it will hold and why, links this policy, and asks you to agree to it separately from the terms, on its own checkbox. We record which version of this policy you agreed to, and when. You can withdraw that consent at any time by deleting your account, which removes the data it covered.

Quebec's Law 25 applies if you live in Quebec. It gives you the same rights described in §10, plus the right to be told before your information is used to make a decision about you based only on automated processing. UPLFT's engine chooses your exercises and loads automatically, which is the whole product, and §4.2 names every conclusion it draws. Nothing it decides affects your credit, employment, insurance, or access to any service. If you ask, we will tell you which of your information was used, the main reasons behind what the app decided, and how to correct anything that was wrong, and you can put your own observations to a person who will review it.

In the United States, most state privacy laws only bite above a size threshold UPLFT is nowhere near. Washington's My Health My Data Act is the exception: it has no threshold at all, and it covers exactly the kind of information UPLFT holds. It requires its own separate policy, so there is one: Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy. It must be linked separately from ours, not buried in this document. Nevada and Connecticut have similar health-data rules, and that policy is written to satisfy them too.

9. Deleting your account

You can delete your account from inside the app: Settings → Delete account. You do not need to email us.

What is deleted: your profile, your email address, your name, your training history, your readiness inputs, your injury flags, your food log, your weigh-ins, your progress photos, your avatar, the screenshots attached to any feedback you sent, and the engine's conclusions about you. Your account itself is removed, so you cannot sign back in, and the copy held on your phone is wiped.

What survives, and why:

  • Payment records, where Canadian tax law requires us to keep them.
  • Your Google Calendar connection, if you made one. Deleting your account does not revoke it. The permission stays live in your Google account and the token stays in your phone's keychain, which outlives deleting the app. You can revoke it yourself at myaccount.google.com/permissions, and you should. We are fixing this so it happens automatically.
  • The text of any feedback, bug report or feature request you sent us, and the email address attached to it. We are fixing this; until we have, it is named here rather than quietly left out of the list above. Ask us and we will remove it by hand.
  • Crash reports already sent to Sentry. These are tagged with your account ID and are not deleted when you delete your account. They age out on Sentry's own 90-day schedule and contain no training data, no photos and no food log.
  • Your subscriber record at RevenueCat, which holds your account ID, your email address and your purchase history. It is not deleted when you delete your account. We keep no payment card details anywhere. Apple handles that and never shares them with us.

We are saying this plainly rather than claiming a clean sweep we do not perform. If you want either of those removed as well, email hello@uplftapp.com and we will do it by hand.

Deleting your account does not cancel your subscription. Apple handles billing, and you have to cancel it in Settings → Apple Account → Subscriptions on your device. We cannot cancel it for you.

10. Your other rights

You can ask us for a copy of everything we hold about you, ask us to correct anything that is wrong, and ask us to stop using your data. Email hello@uplftapp.com and we will respond within 30 days, the standard under PIPEDA.

There is no export button in the app. Ask us and we will send you a copy.

If you live in Quebec, you also have the right to receive the information you gave us in a structured, commonly used technological format, and to have it sent to another organisation. Ask us and we will produce it.

If you live in Washington, Nevada or Connecticut, you have specific rights over health data on top of these. They are set out in the separate Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy.

10a. Who is accountable for this

Canadian privacy law requires a named individual to be accountable for the information an organisation holds, and for the policies that govern it. For UPLFT that person is Jaden Joseph, reachable at hello@uplftapp.com.

UPLFT has no employees and no contractors. Nobody else is accountable, and nobody else has access.

10b. If there is ever a breach

If your information is lost, stolen, or accessed by someone who should not have it, and there is a real risk of significant harm to you, we will:

  • tell the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, as PIPEDA requires;
  • tell you directly, by email, as soon as we reasonably can;
  • tell you what happened, what information was involved, and what you can do about it;
  • keep a record of the breach, which we are required to hold for two years and to produce to the Commissioner on request.

If you are in the United States, the Federal Trade Commission's Health Breach Notification Rule also applies to us. We will tell you without unreasonable delay and within 60 days, notify the FTC, and notify media where that rule requires it.

We will not wait for certainty before telling you. If we know enough to be worried, that is enough to tell you.

11. How we protect it

  • In transit: everything travels over HTTPS.
  • At rest: encrypted by our hosting provider.
  • No passwords: there is no password to steal, because we do not use them.
  • Progress photos and feedback screenshots are private: they sit in storage that is not publicly readable, and reaching them needs a signed link that we issue only to you.
  • Your profile photo is not private. It is stored in a public bucket so the app can display it, which means anyone who has or guesses its address can open it without signing in. The address contains your account ID and is not listed anywhere, but it is not a secret. Do not use a photo for your avatar that you would not be comfortable being public. (Progress photos are not stored this way.)
  • Access: UPLFT is run by one person, Jaden Joseph, who is the only human with access to the database. Nobody else is employed by or contracts for UPLFT.

No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security. What we can say is what we actually do, which is the list above.

12. Permissions the app asks for

Each is asked for when you first use the feature that needs it, not on launch, and the app works without any of them:

  • Camera: to photograph a meal, scan a barcode, or take a progress photo.
  • Photos: to pick an existing photo, and to save a share image.
  • Microphone and speech recognition: to let you say what you ate or what you trained.
  • Motion: to notice when you turn your phone sideways so a demo can fill the screen.
  • Calendar: to add a booked session to your own calendar.
  • Notifications: to remind you. These are scheduled on your device; we do not hold a push token and cannot send you anything remotely.

13. Sharing to other apps

When you share a session image, it is created on your device and handed to iOS's own share sheet. It goes wherever you send it, and from that point it is handled by that app under that company's policy. We never receive a copy and we do not track what you share.

14. Changes to this policy

The date at the top is always accurate. If we make a change that materially affects how we handle your data, we will tell you inside the app before it takes effect, not by quietly editing this page.

15. Complaints

If you are not satisfied with how we have handled your information, you can complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada at www.priv.gc.ca or 1-800-282-1376.

If you live in Quebec, you may complain to the Commission d'accès à l'information du Québec. If you live in Washington State, the My Health My Data Act lets you bring a claim directly under the Consumer Protection Act, and you may complain to the Washington State Attorney General.

We would rather hear from you first, and we will not make you go through a complaint to get your data deleted. That button is in the app.
